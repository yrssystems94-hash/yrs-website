What You're Actually Buying

A roof is a system. Most quotes only price the shingles.

The shingles are the part you can see, and they're a fraction of what keeps water out of your house. When two quotes are thousands of dollars apart, the difference is almost never the shingle brand — it's what the cheaper quote quietly left out. Here's the full scope of a proper replacement:

  • Full tear-off to the deck — every layer of old shingles and underlayment removed and hauled away, so nothing is hidden
  • Decking inspection and repair — rotted or delaminated sheathing found and replaced while it's exposed, which is the only time it can be
  • Ice-and-water protection at the eaves and in every valley — Ontario's building code requires eave protection for a reason, and this is what stops ice-dam backup from reaching your ceiling
  • New underlayment across the full deck, not just the vulnerable areas
  • All new metal flashing at every wall, chimney, skylight and valley — reused flashing is one of the most common sources of a leak in an otherwise new roof
  • Code-compliant ventilation — intake and exhaust balanced properly, because trapped attic heat shortens shingle life and is a leading cause of ice dams in KW
  • New CertainTeed shingles installed to manufacturer specification, with the correct starter and hip-and-ridge components

Skipping any one of these is how a brand-new roof develops the same problems as the old one inside a few years — and how a warranty gets denied when you try to use it.

The Difference That Matters

Why we won't roof over your existing shingles

Laying new shingles on top of old ones is faster, cheaper, and genuinely tempting when you're comparing bids. It's also the single decision that costs you the most later, and there are two hard reasons we don't do it.

First: it disqualifies your warranty. CertainTeed's own installation specification states plainly that a lay-over or roof-over installation does not qualify for extended warranty coverage. If a contractor offers you a "new roof" over the existing one and also promises a long manufacturer warranty, those two things are in conflict.

Second: it hides the part that actually fails. The decking is what your entire roof is fastened to. Covering it up means nobody has looked at it — and if there's rot under there, you've just paid to seal it in. On top of that, the top-tier CertainTeed warranty requires all metal flashings to be replaced, including step, sidewall, chimney and skylight flashing. A roof-over reuses them by definition.

Doing it properly costs more up front. That's the honest trade, and it's the one we'd want made on our own house.

When replacement is genuinely the right call

We'd rather repair your roof than replace it, and we'll tell you when a repair is all you need. Replacement is the honest recommendation when the damage isn't contained to one area — widespread granule loss and curling across the field of the roof, multiple leak points rather than one, a roof already past the 20 to 30 years an asphalt system lasts in Ontario, or a situation where you've paid for repairs more than once and they keep moving to the next weak spot.

At that point repairs stop being economical, because you're maintaining a system that's finished. We diagnose the actual condition first and show you photos of what we found, so the recommendation is something you can see rather than something you have to take on faith.

Quick Answers

Roof replacement questions

Can you install new shingles over my existing roof?

We don't, and there's a specific reason. Laying new shingles over old ones is faster and cheaper up front, but CertainTeed's own installation specification states that a lay-over or roof-over installation does not qualify for extended warranty coverage. It also hides whatever condition the decking underneath is actually in. Every roof replacement we do is a full tear-off to the deck.

What's actually included in a roof replacement quote?

Full tear-off and disposal of the old roofing, inspection of the decking with any rotted sheathing replaced, ice-and-water protection at the eaves and valleys, new underlayment, new metal flashing at every wall, chimney, skylight and valley, code-compliant ventilation, and new CertainTeed shingles installed to manufacturer specification. If a quote you're comparing doesn't itemize these, ask which ones it leaves out.

What shingles do you install?

CertainTeed Landmark is our primary shingle. It's an architectural laminate shingle built for freeze-thaw climates, and our crew holds CertainTeed Master Shingle Applicator, Master Craftsman, and ShingleMaster Pro credentials. Other CertainTeed lines and colours are available depending on your home and budget.

What determines the cost of a roof replacement?

Roof size measured in squares, pitch and how steep it is to work safely, the number of existing layers to tear off, the condition of the decking once it's exposed, the number of valleys, dormers, chimneys and skylights that need flashing, access for equipment and disposal, and the shingle line you choose. We measure the roof and give you a specific number in writing, not a range that moves later.

How long does a roof replacement take?

Most residential replacements are completed in one to a few days depending on roof size, how many layers come off, and weather. You get a specific timeline before the crew arrives, not a vague window.

Do I need to be home during the replacement?

No. Most homeowners aren't home for the full job. We protect the property and landscaping, handle all disposal, and document the work with photos so you can see exactly what was found and what was done.
