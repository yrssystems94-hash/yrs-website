Try It On Your Roof

See it before you commit

Choosing a roof colour from a brochure is guesswork, because a two-inch chip and two thousand square feet of roof are not the same experience. CertainTeed's ColorView tool solves most of that: you upload a photo of your own house and it puts real shingle colours onto your actual roofline, with your brick, your trim and your landscaping in frame.

It's free, and it's the manufacturer's own tool rather than a third-party approximation — the colours in it are the colours we'd install.

One thing to know before you click: CertainTeed asks you to create a free account before you can upload a photo of your home. It takes a minute and costs nothing, but we'd rather tell you now than have you find out three screens in.

Once you have it down to two or three, that is the point to get us out. The estimate is free, we bring the real samples, and holding them against your own brick in daylight settles it faster than any screen will.

Choosing One

What actually decides whether a colour works

After enough roofs you stop thinking about which colour is nicest and start thinking about which one won't look wrong in five years. A few things matter more than people expect.

Your brick decides more than your taste does

Most of the housing stock across Kitchener-Waterloo is warm red or brown brick. The colours that tend to sit comfortably against it are the warm-neutral blends — Weathered Wood, Resawn Shake, Heather Blend, Burnt Sienna. Cool greys can absolutely work, but against warm brick they sometimes read cold or faintly purple under overcast light, and overcast is most of our winter. If your house is grey brick, stone or a painted finish, the greys open right up.

A chip in your hand lies to you

Architectural shingles are a blend of several granule colours. At arm's length you see the individual granules; at street level they average out. The practical effect is that almost every shingle reads lighter and more uniform on the roof than it does in your hand. If you're torn between two shades of the same colour, the darker one is usually the one that gives you what the sample looked like.

Pitch changes the colour

A steep roof faces you and reflects more sky; a shallow roof is seen at a glancing angle and reflects more of what's above it. The same shingle genuinely looks different on a Westmount century home than on a bungalow, which is why we look at samples on your roof rather than in the driveway.

Look at it twice, at two times of day

Midday sun and late-afternoon light do very different things to a granule blend. If you're deciding between two, look at both at noon and again around dusk before you commit. It's the cheapest possible way to avoid a decision you'll look at for the next twenty-five years.

One thing to check if you are in Beechwood

A few of the Beechwood neighbourhoods in Waterloo have old rules attached to the property from when they were first built. Nearly always those associations just look after the shared pools and clubhouses and have nothing at all to say about your roof — but if you are not sure whether yours covers the outside of the house, it is worth a quick look before you sign anything with anybody. We are happy to wait while you check.

Quick Answers

Roof colour questions

Can I see what a roof colour looks like on my own house?

Yes. CertainTeed's ColorView tool lets you upload a photo of your own home and try shingle colours on it, and it's free to use. One thing worth knowing before you start: CertainTeed asks you to create a free account before you can upload a photo. It takes a minute and costs nothing. Once you have a shortlist, book a free estimate and we will bring the real samples to hold against your brick.

What roof colours do you install?

We install the CertainTeed Landmark range, which runs to 10 colours available here, plus Landmark PRO in ten Max Def colours and Landmark ClimateFlex in six. Landmark is our standard for most homes. Availability moves with the supply chain, so confirm your final choice with us before you settle on it rather than after.

Can I change my mind about the colour after I have booked?

Yes, right up until we order materials, and there is no cost to it — just tell us as soon as you know. Once the order is placed a change means restocking, which we would have to charge for, so we always confirm your colour with you in writing before anything is ordered.

Which roof colour works with red brick?

Most of the housing stock here is warm red or brown brick, and the colours that tend to sit comfortably against it are the warm-neutral blends — Weathered Wood, Resawn Shake, Heather Blend, Burnt Sienna. Cool greys can work, but against warm brick they sometimes read cold or slightly purple in overcast light, which is most of our winter. The safest test is to hold a physical sample against your own brick outdoors, not to compare them on a screen.

Why does the colour look different on my roof than on the sample?

Two reasons. Architectural shingles are a blend of several granule colours, so at arm's length you see the individual granules and at street level you see them average out — which almost always reads lighter and more uniform than the chip in your hand. And roof pitch changes how much sky reflects off the surface, so the same shingle looks different on a steep roof than on a shallow one. This is exactly why we bring physical samples and look at them on site.

Do I have to choose a colour before I get a quote?

No. The estimate is free and doesn't depend on the colour, and within the Landmark range the colour you pick doesn't change the price. Get the number first, then take your time over the colour — most people shortlist two or three and decide once they've seen samples against the house.
